## Prince Lodin (Ventrue, Chicago)

**The Dictator**. Autocratic ruler of Chicago. Ventrue. A man who came to power a century ago and has spent every decade since crushing anyone foolish enough to challenge him.

A century of crushing rivals and the Anarch Movement has left Lodin absolutely dominant in his domain. He is feared, paranoid, and obsessed with control. Chicago is his masterpiece—the Camarilla's shining city on the shore of Lake Michigan, a jewel of apparent stability and prosperity. From boardrooms and artistic salons, from corporate offices and university boards, he orchestrates both mortal and Kindred power structures with surgical precision. Every major institution in Chicago traces back to Lodin's interests.

**Key Fact**: Lodin is the reason Gary is dying.

In the 1980s, Lodin orchestrated the collapse of Gary's steel industry and the destruction of union power—not as a side effect, but as the deliberate execution of a rival. His target was Modius, a Toreador Prince of Gary whose only real sin was existing in Lodin's shadow. Lodin manipulated corporate boardrooms and Republican politicians from a distance, maneuvering mortal institutions like chess pieces. The steel mills closed. The unions broke. Hundreds of thousands of mortals lost jobs, families, and everything they had. Many ended their own lives rather than face the wreckage. But Modius was broken, and Gary was transformed into a lawless no-man's-land nominally outside Lodin's direct control.

**In 1991**: Lodin is paranoid about Modius's residual power and increasingly concerned about Anarch infiltration of Gary. He maintains the convenient fiction that Gary is Modius's domain (allowing the broken Toreador to preserve face), but everyone understands the reality: Lodin has abandoned the city to decay as punishment, and as containment.

## Modius (Toreador, Gary)

**The Failed Rival**. Prince of Gary. Once ambitious. Now broken and very much diminished.

Modius is a Toreador aesthete who spent a century trying to outshine Lodin—his superior in every conceivable way that matters in Camarilla politics. Where Lodin possessed superior political acumen, corporate connections, and favored status within the Sect, Modius had only ego, artistic pretension, and jealousy.

**The Destruction**: In the 1980s, Lodin destroyed Modius's power base without ever confronting him directly. No assassination. No open war. Just the deliberate, patient dismantling of everything Modius ruled. The steel mills that produced his wealth. The unions that comprised his power base. Both gone. It was a masterclass in political annihilation delivered over the course of a decade. Modius watched his domain crumble and was helpless to stop it. His court in Gary still exists, but it is a shadow—a theater production where the audience knows it's all pretense.

**In 1991**: Modius is broken but not yet destroyed. He is still a danger—still prideful, still cunning, still willing to take risks. Most dangerously, he is increasingly desperate. A desperate vampire is an unpredictable vampire.

Modius nominally claims to rule Gary, and the Camarilla allows him this fiction because it costs Lodin nothing and preserves the Sect's appearance of stability. But everyone knows Modius rules a dead city, and everyone knows *why*.

**Strategic Significance**: Modius is the backdoor. He is willing to empower Neonates in ways that Lodin would never permit. A young vampire who proves valuable to Modius might find themselves with real power in Gary—and with a very dangerous patron who has every reason to use them against his hated rival. Modius has nothing to lose and everything to gain by creating problems for Lodin.

## The Anarchs of Gary

While Modius maintains the pretense of Camarilla authority, Gary is increasingly a haven for Anarch operations. The lawlessness. The mortal desperation. The absence of organized Camarilla enforcement. All of it attracts revolutionary vampires like blood in the water.

The Anarchs in Gary are less organized than their West Coast counterparts but growing in number and confidence. They see Gary as a beachhead—a foothold from which to infiltrate Chicago itself, to prove that Lodin's autocracy can be challenged, that the Masquerade's price can be refused.

Lodin knows this. He watches. And he waits.

---

## The Nexus

These three forces—Lodin's absolute control in Chicago, Modius's broken rule in Gary, and the Anarchs' growing presence—define the political landscape in which the chronicle begins.

Our characters enter this world as newly-embraced Neonates abandoned in Gary's no-man's-land. Too young to matter to Lodin. Too useful to ignore for Modius. Too valuable as potential recruits for the Anarchs.

All three powers will eventually take notice. The question is not whether, but when.
