Known as "the Hangman's Bride," Maryann has extensively burned skin, milky eyes, a wig and a walking stick. Her movement is restricted and her speech comes in short, effortful bursts. Her investigations can expose princes and elders as well as neonates.

## At the Act II ending

In [The Signed Discharge](/posts/the-signed-discharge/), Maryann records the coterie's account of [Lodin](/npcs/lodin/)'s deniable commission and takes [Neally Edwards](/npcs/neally-edwards/) as a voluntary witness. She confirms the limits of [Nicolai](/npcs/nicolai/)'s examination and preserves both the commission and the Prince's objection on the record.

Her cemetery observer is the chronicler in the gray overcoat. This does not identify the separate gray car at Walt's walkup. At the closing scene she has a police photograph from Kedzie Avenue and asks all three PCs where they fed the previous night. The question remains unanswered; no confession or finding against a PC has occurred.

Her Chicago visit is this chronicle's placement. Her early twentieth-century appointment supports service under the living Petrodon in 1992; later patronless status is not backdated.

Background: *Children of the Revolution*, pp. 75-76, and the [Kindred Codex entry](https://kindred-codex.pages.dev/kindred/#3335). Campaign position: [Acts I & II wrap-up](/acts-1-2-wrap-up/).
