The man in the lavender tie. American suit, American shoes, American tan. The professional stillness of someone who already knows how the evening ends and is letting the clock run.

First spotted as a dark sedan two cars back on Lake Shore Drive, maintaining professional spacing. He tailed [Lodin](/npcs/lodin/)'s motorcade to O'Hare, placed a crude bomb under the Learjet's starboard wing — defective by design, meant to be found, meant to signal that someone knew about the trip.

At the Piccadilly auction, Quinn entered outrageous bids on Lot 47 — Admiral Tourney's journal containing a fragment reference to the Book of Nod. He tripled every counter, smiled at [Roarke](/npcs/roarke/) like a letter addressed personally, then walked out past his chair close enough for [Roarke](/npcs/roarke/) to smell the aftershave. American. Expensive.

After the auction, Quinn circled back and waited in the shadows with a suppressed rifle. When Roarke returned alone to collect the journal, Quinn shot him center-mass on the pavement. He leaned close, picked up the journal, and said: "[Lodin](/npcs/lodin/) said he would send you out on your own sometime during this trip. I just had to wait for the right time."

Then he uncorked a vial of ancient blood — from the same source that would later transform Roarke — and fed it to the dying man. Not enough to wake him. Enough to keep the candle lit.

Quinn is not Kindred. He serves a master whose identity is never revealed in Ashes to Ashes, but who possesses blood old enough to taste like time itself.
