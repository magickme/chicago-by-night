*New Orleans, Louisiana / October 19, 1991*

---

The figure was gone by the time they came back from the service hatch. Luc took the lead through the ovens, with Calvi and Constanzo behind him. At the fire tunnel he lowered himself into the passage and found fresh marks in the soot, heading toward the far door. A flare lit the bricks ahead of him. He backed out before it reached him.

They had talked about using one of the flamethrowers themselves. Luc went back across the yard to the hatch and examined the damaged equipment in the service room. He worked a jammed control loose on the second unit, but there was too little fuel in its tank, and the full tank from the first would not fit. He left them where they were. At the tunnel, Calvi and Constanzo had heard two voices on the other side. Neither could make out the words.

Luc crossed alone when the passage went dark. Two figures were waiting in the chamber beyond. One retreated while the other came at him. Luc caught the one who stayed, tore him down, and went after the other. Behind him, the flame returned before the vampires could follow. Constanzo fled into the chamber; Calvi held at the entrance until the flare passed. Neither had been burned.

The runner disappeared among the oven doors. Luc followed a sound to the wrong opening, then worked his way along the brick, listening. One iron door opened under his hand. The air behind it smelled of damp masonry and fresh blood. He saw something move near the floor inside, but when he went in, it was gone. A smear led to a narrow crack at the back of the oven.

Luc tested the brick, then broke through it. Beyond the broken face was a flue, too narrow for him to enter and bending upward out of sight. A draft came down it. He listened, with his muzzle near the opening, but could hear nothing above. The blood was strongest at the lip; it gave him no trail farther on.

He left the oven and found a heavy steel door in a short brick approach. One side of its frame shifted when he tested it. He wedged a loose iron bar into the gap and put his weight on it. The bar slipped, driving his shoulder against the door. Somewhere beyond it, footsteps moved. Luc stepped aside and waited, claws ready. The door stayed shut, and the footsteps receded.

He returned to the jamb. Brick cracked around the frame under the bar, and the steel door gave way.

Two guards stood inside with tanks on their backs and nozzles in their hands. Behind them, a short blond man held a sword. The guards raised their weapons as Luc came through. Fire struck him before he could reach them. His skin resisted some of the heat, but the next burst found him, and then another as he tried to tear a nozzle apart.

He abandoned the nozzle and went for its bearer. One guard fell under his claws. Luc crossed the room to the other, who tried to get clear, and brought him down as well. The blond man had kept his distance. When Luc turned toward him, arms of darkness reached out of the room. One caught at him and slipped away.

The man looked at Luc and spoke a single word.

"Stop."

Luc stopped. The swordsman approached while the shadow arms moved around him. One tried to wrap Luc and failed; the next time the man came close, he used the sword. Luc fell where the guards had fallen. His body changed back to human shape on the floor.

Calvi and Constanzo had been listening at the other end of the tunnel. They could hear blows somewhere beyond it, muffled by the brick. Calvi went through first while the passage was clear. Constanzo followed, but fire flared across the far end before she could reach him. She held herself at the entrance and waited for it to die.

Calvi went on alone. The steel door stood open. Inside, he found Luc on the floor and the two guards nearby. He could not see the man with the sword. Calvi knelt beside Luc and drank from him. The blood restored what he had spent healing, and it left him watching the dark with a new suspicion. A scrape sounded from deeper in the room.

At the tunnel entrance, Constanzo looked for a control she could reach. There was soot around the slots, but no valve or fuel line on her side of the wall. The flame returned at the far end while she searched. The service room could still be reached from the yard and the hatch, though she could not tell whether either of its damaged units was feeding the fire.

Calvi followed the sound through the room. At first he found nothing. Then he saw the swordsman standing beyond Luc. A shadow reached for Calvi, and he sent one of his own across the floor. It rose around the man's body and held him. More arms moved in the dark beside him.

Calvi tightened his grip. The man struggled, but the coils held. When Calvi asked whether he was Carl Wisor, the prisoner gave no answer. The pressure of the shadow did not make him speak, and it did not knock him senseless.

Calvi meant to bring him back. The man tore free of the first hold, and Calvi caught his eye.

"Stop."

The man obeyed long enough for Calvi's shadow to seize him again. By then another of the dark arms had closed around Calvi. It squeezed until he managed to wrench himself free, leaving him hurt and short of room. He tried to command the swordsman to dismiss the arms. Before the order took hold, another coil caught him.

They remained facing one another across the chamber, each held by the other's darkness. Luc's body lay between them and the broken door. Constanzo was still on the other side of the fire tunnel.
