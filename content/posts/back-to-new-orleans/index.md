*New Orleans, Louisiana / October 13–19, 1991*

---

Sangris had recognized the work on the prisoner's face in Montreal. Someone had shaped it to match the photograph the pack had been given. Back in New Orleans, they held the double down and pulled the stake so he could speak. Constanzo heard him describe the real Carl Wisor: short, slender, blond. He could tell them what his sire looked like. He couldn't tell them where he was.

The man who followed them through the Quarter ran when Calvi's shadows reached for him. They lost him, picked up his trail, and caught him on foot. Calvi told him to stop. He stopped, though he could still speak.

"Tully Jones. Brujah."

Constanzo asked who he reported to. "Nobody. I don't take orders."

He had been watching them because they were hunting a friend of his. They kept him there until he told them about the abandoned brickyard off the interstate, the ovens underground, and the guards inside.

A witness went for the police. Calvi held Tully with another word while Constanzo fed. Tully's veins were empty by then. She kept her mouth on him until she took his soul. By the time the officers came up the street, Puente was holding Constanzo in a doorway and Calvi was waiting for them. The night left more bodies behind it. The brickyard lead was still good.

A flashlight swung across the yard when they arrived. The man holding it called out and ran for the ovens. Puente caught him before he got there. His wallet gave them a name, Etienne LeBlanc, and nothing about who paid him. They took the light inside.

The tunnel was low enough that they had to stoop. When the guardians came out of the dark, the pack fell back toward the entrance and fought them there. Deeper in, the brick opened into a round chamber with metal oven doors. Calvi carried the flashlight into the next passage. The doors slammed at both ends before the others were through, and fire came out of the walls.

Calvi threw himself against the near door from inside. Constanzo and Puente worked it from the chamber. It took all three of them to force it. Calvi got clear badly burned, and the fire kept coming in bursts. The other oven doors gave them no way around it. Puente could hear pressure in the lines behind the brick.

She and Constanzo waited for the hiss to fall away, then crossed to the far door. They hit it together. It held. When the jets came back, Constanzo fled toward the open end. Puente stayed at the far door. The catch gave a little under her weight. She hit it again in the fire and moved it a little farther. She died at the door.

Constanzo found Calvi in the chamber. She helped him crawl past the pit and out of the ovens. At the Ninth Ward rental, she found blood for him. They returned to the yard after several nights of recovery.

This time they came down a service hatch and found the equipment behind the slots. Constanzo got close enough to pull a fuel line off one unit while its operator was still there. By the time they climbed out, both operators were dead, both lines were off, and the controls were bent. They went back through the ovens and put their shoulders into the door Puente had damaged. It opened.

The hungry vampires in the next room came at them in the beam of the flashlight. Calvi's shadows held some of them while others pressed in. The flashlight broke in the fight. They left two of the attackers down and felt their way back through the fire passage, with more coming behind them. Constanzo helped Calvi along the pit's edge in the dark.

A car was watching the rental. When Calvi spotted a tail, they split up. Constanzo went to Jo, who got word to a Gangrel with contacts among the Black Spiral Dancers. The man they met through him agreed to scout the brickyard and help them look for Wisor. His people wanted two thousand dollars, half up front. They also wanted a say in what happened to anything unusual found inside.

The Dancers found them a guarded place to sleep. Calvi paid the advance. The vampires moved their gear, their earth, and the staked double out of the Ninth Ward. After a day under cover, Calvi had healed enough to go back. The Dancer gave his name on the next hunt. Luc listened while they told him what the fire had done and how little room there was to move underground.

At the brickyard someone had put a new padlock on the hatch. The lookout turned toward a sound in the yard. By the time they had him held, another man had come out of a weathered doorway. Luc changed shape and killed him. Calvi and Constanzo drank from the captive outside while someone threw a brick at Luc from the building.

They went in and took another man alive long enough to ask who paid him. He said he had been given cash to watch the storage room door. He couldn't give them a name. Luc killed him when they were done, and they went back to the hatch.

Calvi helped Luc tear the hasp loose. When Calvi raised the lid, somebody shoved it back against him. Luc swiped at the hand and missed. Then he went down through the opening and killed the person below, and the one who came at him after. He checked the room as far as he could see.

Calvi and Constanzo dropped down beside him. Somebody had put one of the fuel lines back on. Luc ripped it loose and crushed the control. The second line was still off. His claws slipped on its housing when he tried to break it, and the clang carried through the brick.

A silhouette moved beyond the slots. From the service room they could see it pass through the fire tunnel. Behind them, the hatch was still open.
