*[Astor](/npcs/lady-astor/) Street Chantry / The Drake / [Maryann Fletcher](/npcs/maryann-fletcher/)'s temporary office*
*Chicago, Illinois*

---

[Nicolai](/npcs/nicolai/) dated the finding and signed beneath it. Across the table, [Neally Edwards](/npcs/neally-edwards/) scraped dried blood from his wrist with a thumbnail. Churchyard soil lodged under the nail and fell onto the Chantry's table.

"You've had your reading, Cole. I came back as promised. Now I want to leave."

[Darius](/darius-cole/) watched Nicolai reach for the telephone. [Sable](/sable-price/) stood beside the table; Tomás held the other side. Neally had returned from the church on the terms they had agreed, and Nicolai had found what [Lodin](/npcs/lodin/) required. Foster's hold was gone. Whatever had happened in the cellar belonged to the people who had been inside it.

Nicolai asked for [Thornhill](/npcs/brennon-thornhill/) and drew the signed page closer. He reported the result while Neally waited at the door. When the Prince's answer came, Nicolai covered the mouthpiece.

"The Drake. Eighth floor, within the hour. All three of you. Edwards accompanies you alive."

Neally came back to the table. There was still an order to kill him in circulation; Lodin's men had tried to take him from the storage unit the previous night. He wanted [Bach](/npcs/bach/). He wanted someone else to carry the Prince's message.

Nicolai held the receiver out to Darius.

"We're bringing him," Darius said. He looked at Neally. "If he tries to leave, we stake him."

Sable cleared a route toward the stairs. Tomás stayed beside Edwards. Neally studied the distance between them, then asked for the Prince's protection while under his roof. Nicolai passed the condition to Thornhill without promising it himself. He folded the finding into an envelope.

"[Jefferson Foster](/npcs/jefferson-foster/)'s Blood Bond no longer holds Edwards. Anyone requiring a broader conclusion can ask me."

In the back of the borrowed van, Neally's shoes shed earth across the floor. Tomás drove. Sable sat close enough to stop Edwards reaching the door. Darius kept the envelope inside his coat and counted the people who would have to accept its contents before this was finished.

***

Brennon met them at the elevators. Upstairs, someone had moved the chairs away from the long table. Two men waited near an adjoining door; the open carpet between them and Lodin gave anyone entering several paces in which to be inspected.

Neally asked for the Hunt to be withdrawn. Lodin asked Darius for Nicolai's finding.

"Foster had my administrator bound to him," the Prince said. "You have now produced evidence that such a bond can be broken. I intend to put that evidence before the Primogen."

Neally turned toward Darius. Sable's sleeve touched his as she moved closer.

Lodin continued. "There are Kindred in this city who have resisted giving me the same assurance of loyalty they freely demand from their own servants. Foster's experiment gives us something useful to discuss."

Darius put the envelope on the table. Lodin opened it and checked the signature. The party that had performed the rite had kept them outside. They could attest to the return and the examination. They had no procedure to teach, no means of offering the Primogen release from anything Lodin chose to impose.

"Withdraw Edwards's Hunt," Darius said. "Acknowledge that Cole, Price and [Navarro](/npcs/elena-navarro/) fulfilled your commission. Then we account for the agreement."

Lodin put the finding flat on the table. "Breaking Foster's hold does not settle what Edwards did while under it."

"State those charges separately. We have delivered the result you required."

He kept his hands still. He had brought a child to the Prince and left without her. He had taken this man out of the storage unit and now delivered him to his sire, and could hear his own voice making the arrangements. Sable remained beside him. Tomás watched the men by the door. Darius held to the three names and the completed commission until Lodin drew hotel stationery toward himself.

"Cole, Price, and Navarro have fulfilled the commission I gave them concerning Edwards. The finding will enter the court record under all three names."

The pen moved beneath the acknowledgment. Lodin signed and handed it to Brennon.

"The Lextalionis against Neally Edwards is withdrawn. Notify the Sheriff. Circulate the revocation tonight. Give Cole a copy."

Darius then described the price: Foster wanted an introduction in Colorado, through Darius, conditional on Edwards returning free of the old bond. No names had passed. Neally had told a story about someone who could restore a vampire's humanity. Darius had offered contact with people who had investigated it. He had promised no cure.

"Make no approach to Denver tonight," Lodin said. "I will speak to Nicolai."

Brennon returned with the signed copy. Sable put two fingers against its edge, keeping it clear of the other papers.

"The agreement was made to complete your commission," she said.

"And the commission is complete. I have acknowledged it."

Neally stepped toward Darius and Tomás. "Then take me back."

Lodin stood. One of the men moved into the route to the hall.

"Edwards remains here."

He was still Lodin's childe. The Prince said so, then pointed to a chair. Darius sat within reach of the signed sheet. Neally sat opposite him with his coat on and his feet drawn beneath the seat. Tomás remained standing at the end of the table.

Brennon's telephone sounded in the adjoining room. When he returned he carried credentials for a representative of the Justicar waiting downstairs. Lodin read them, creasing the edge under his thumb, and ordered the visitor admitted. Sable drew the discharge out from among his papers and placed it in front of Darius.

***

Maryann Fletcher entered with a walking stick planted ahead of her. The skin beneath her wig had been burned into ridges; the movement of her jaw pulled at the scars on her neck. Milky eyes passed over the table. Behind her came the man in the gray overcoat who had watched the cemetery, his ledger against his chest.

"Archon to Justicar Petrodon," Brennon said.

Maryann set her case beside the attestation. "Edwards's testimony. The examiner's finding. Statements from these three."

Lodin told her the operation had succeeded under his authority. She read Nicolai's page, then asked who had examined Edwards for instructions left behind. Nicolai would have to answer that. She would speak to him tonight.

The chronicler opened his ledger to the church grounds. Several names and a rough plan occupied the page. Maryann required the terms of the handover. Neally told her about Colorado and his intended return to the pack. Lodin put a hand on his shoulder.

"Remove your hand from the witness," she said.

Sable eased her chair back. Tomás kept the entrance in view. Darius slid the signed discharge toward Maryann and stated the commission, including the rite Lodin had required. He asked her to take Neally's account in the room, with everyone present.

Brennon went to reach the Chantry. The chronicler turned to a clean page. Neally named Lorna and identified the men at the storage unit as Lodin's security. Lodin defended their recovery of a fugitive under his decree. The pen continued across the page.

Maryann asked Neally what he had undertaken during the rite. He told her Foster had released him. She asked what he had agreed to give, then why he wanted to return to the pack.

"They took me in."

"As what?"

"One of their own."

A guard reached inside his jacket. Maryann raised her palm and ordered him to keep the weapon holstered. Lodin walked around Neally's chair.

"You accepted membership in the Sabbat."

Neally stood, catching the chair against Lodin's leg. He had accepted the people who got him out of Foster's hold. The guard crossed the doorway. Sable rose beside Darius.

"Cole. Keep him in that chair," Lodin said.

Maryann held the corner of the acknowledgment against the table. "Your commission is recorded. Now we establish what each of you did."

"Ask what you need," Darius said.

Brennon brought her a chair. She lowered herself with both hands on the head of her stick, then asked what Lodin had told them the rite would do beyond breaking the bond. Any warning. Any condition concerning Edwards's allegiance afterward.

Darius gave her the pumping-station instructions. Sabbat knowledge and a willing subject. Lodin had said Edwards would return "no longer bound to anyone." Thirty days to return him functional and unbound. Operational latitude; the Prince had said he did not wish to know about their contacts. They had secured a hearing, negotiated the return, and waited outside. Darius had accepted the Colorado undertaking. He had received no instruction for a man who chose the pack afterward.

"You were expected to exercise judgment," Lodin said.

"We brought him back. We obtained the examination. We brought him here."

Maryann asked whether the Prince disputed that account. He disputed the implication that he had authorized Edwards to join the Sabbat. Her chronicler recorded the objection beside the commission.

Nicolai confirmed his finding by telephone. He offered no finding on allegiance. Lodin ordered Edwards held under guard; Maryann required those guards outside. When Lodin told her whose orders they took, she braced the stick against the carpet and stood.

"Then give them the order."

She would continue her examination elsewhere and report Lodin's cooperation to Petrodon. Lodin told Brennon to summon the Sheriff. Sable moved close to Darius's ear.

"We have the signed discharge. We should go while she's still here."

Darius folded it into his coat. Tomás made room at the doorway. The guard looked toward the table.

"Let them through," Lodin said.

Brennon held the elevator. Nicolai's attestation remained under the Prince's hand as the doors closed.

***

The kitchen corridor smelled of dishwater and hot fat. A porter rolled a cart past them. Beyond the swinging doors, crockery broke and someone swore. Tomás went ahead to the van, then stopped with the keys in his hand.

The driver's quarter window was broken. Glass covered the seat. A wet streak ran down the sliding door, and the smell of gasoline reached Darius at the threshold.

Sable caught Neally's sleeve. Maryann drew back against the brick jamb.

A gray sedan stood near the mouth of the lane, driver's door open. [Sullivan Dane](/npcs/sullivan-dane/) came out behind it in a long coat and gloves, burn scars visible above a cuff. His pistol hung beside his right leg. An unlit road flare occupied his left hand.

"Cole. Keep away from the vehicle."

Maryann ordered Tomás inside. Dane raised the pistol toward him.

Darius drew the Colt and fired through the doorway. The report struck the brick walls and came back at him. Dane's coat jerked at his side; his gun swung clear of Tomás as he folded behind the sedan door. The flare stayed dark.

Tomás dropped the keys and ran. His shoulder struck the jamb. Inside, he pulled his pistol and took the opposite side of the entrance. Sable got Neally back along the corridor; the chronicler helped Maryann past the opening. The porter abandoned his cart. Dane's shoe showed beneath the car door, blood on the pavement where he had stood.

His pistol appeared around the rear edge of the door. Darius fired again. The bullet caught Dane above the hip and drove him against the driver's seat. A return shot chipped brick beside Darius's shoulder, scattering grit down his coat. Tomás ducked against the other jamb.

Dane dropped the flare. It rolled beneath the open door as he hauled his leg into the car. The engine caught. Darius lowered the Colt to the nearer rear wheel and fired. Escaping air hissed across the lane. The sidewall folded and the sedan began to thump backward, its door still open.

A taxi crossed the entrance. Dane braked, crouching across the front seat. The taxi cleared the entrance. Darius had an angle on the driver's side window. He fired through it. Glass broke across the upholstery. Dane stayed below the window line. The engine rose and the sedan reversed into the street. A horn sounded beyond the lane. Dane changed gear and drove out of sight, the ruined tire hammering against the pavement.

Darius kept the Colt at the doorway. The van stood under the service light, gasoline dripping from its sliding-door rail. The flare lay unlit. Behind him, Sable had Neally on his feet. Maryann waited at the kitchen doors.

"Cole. Bring your people inside. I need them alive to give their statements."

He stopped to collect the brass. Three casings lay by the jamb. The fourth had rolled under the porter's cart; he crouched and reached beneath its lower shelf while Tomás held the entrance. All four went into his coat pocket. The keys remained by the van in the broken glass.

They passed through the kitchen, staff pressing against the counters to let Maryann through. Someone reported shots into a wall telephone. At the restaurant entrance the air changed to perfume and cooled upholstery. A bellman carried luggage toward the elevators. Guests kept arriving through the revolving doors.

Maryann's second retainer brought her car to the front. Neally rode with her and the chronicler. Darius took a taxi with Sable and Tomás and followed them south. He kept one hand against the folded discharge. The brass shifted lower in the same coat whenever the cab turned.

***

The office was a ground-floor meeting room in a smaller hotel. Maryann's retainers checked the corridor before bringing Edwards in. Her chronicler recorded the withdrawn Hunt and Neally's agreement to remain for questioning. Neally read the entry and signed it. Maryann gave Darius a written acknowledgment of the transfer.

Neally supplied the church's telephone number. Darius called with Sable and Tomás beside him.

Bach wanted to know where Edwards was. Darius gave him the sequence: Nicolai's examination, Lodin's summons, the signed revocation, Maryann's custody. Bach required Neally on the telephone.

Edwards stood to speak. He told Bach he had declared himself. They had not touched him. "She wants my statement. I'm giving it."

He listened, looked at Maryann, and handed back the receiver.

"He says he's staying," Bach said. "That's his answer. Foster still gets what you promised."

"The introduction goes through me. No names have been passed."

Foster came to the telephone and asked whether Nicolai was satisfied. Darius told him about the signature. The undertaking concerning Denver remained. Foster required him to keep it. Bach returned and supplied a number for Barb, the daylight contact who could relay arrangements after the pack left the church. Their actual departure would be their own business. Darius wrote down the number.

Edwards returned to his chair, leaving dried earth on the cushion. Sable sat beside Darius. Tomás pulled up the third chair. Maryann had the call entered in the ledger and put the signed papers in order.

She opened another folder. A police photograph showed a porch on Kedzie Avenue, an ambulance stretcher at the foot of the steps. One hand protruded from beneath the sheet. Darius recognized the torn cuff of Andrzej's quilted jacket.

Maryann laid the photograph where all three could see it.

"This death is among the incidents under investigation."

Her milky eyes turned toward their end of the table.

"I need your accounts of where you fed last night."
